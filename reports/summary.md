# Employee Attrition Analytics - Summary of Findings

> **Important:** the workforce data used here is **synthetic** (generated by `src/generate_data.py`). The patterns below show how the analysis works and what its output looks like; they are not findings about any real organisation. Replace `data/employees.csv` with a real extract (same columns) and re-run the pipeline to get real results.

## 1. What was analysed

- **Population:** 2,600 employees who were employed at some point between 1 Jan 2023 and 30 Sep 2026 (1,035 exits, 1,565 still active).
- **Fields used:** department, role, job level, hire and exit dates, tenure, pay position (compa-ratio = salary / level midpoint), performance rating, weekly hours and overtime.
- **Privacy:** only an anonymous employee ID is kept. No names, ages, gender, addresses or contact details are stored.
- **Main metric:** annualised attrition rate = exits per 100 person-years of exposure. This treats people observed for different lengths of time fairly. Monthly rate = exits in the month / average headcount in the month.

## 2. Data quality

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Duplicate rows | 5 found and removed |
| Missing performance rating | 52 rows (2.0%) - kept, labelled "Unrated" |
| Missing weekly hours | 39 rows (1.5%) - filled with the median |
| Exit date before hire date | 0 |
| Exit flag disagrees with exit date | 0 |
| Out-of-range pay or hours | 0 |

## 3. Headline numbers

- Overall attrition is **20.0% a year** (1.7% a month on average over the last 12 months). The trailing-12-month rate moved only slightly: 19.1% (Dec 2023), 19.7% (Dec 2024), 20.5% (Dec 2025), 20.4% (Sep 2026).
- **5.6%** of recent hires left within 90 days and **27.7%** within 12 months.

## 4. Strongest patterns

1. **Pay position is the strongest signal.** Attrition falls steadily as pay moves up the range: 32.8% a year for people paid below 0.90 of their level midpoint, 22.4% (0.90-1.00), 16.4% (1.00-1.10) and 12.3% above 1.10. After adjusting for other factors, each +0.1 in compa-ratio is associated with a **27% lower** exit rate (rate ratio 0.73, 95% CI 0.70-0.78).
2. **The first two years are the risky period.** Compared with people with 5+ years, the exit rate is about 2.4 times higher at 6-12 months (CI 1.96-2.88), 1.8 times at 0-6 months and 1.5 times at 1-2 years. After year two the difference is no longer statistically significant.
3. **Sales and Customer Support lose the most people.** Sales runs at 27.2% a year and Customer Support at 24.5%, against 12.7% in Finance. Adjusted for the other factors, Sales has a 55% higher exit rate than Engineering (CI 1.31-1.85) and Customer Support 35% higher (CI 1.12-1.62). Sales (34.3%) and Product (36.4%, only 33 hires, so very uncertain) have the highest 12-month early-exit rates.
4. **Low performers leave faster.** Staff rated 1-2 leave at 23.8% a year versus 19.1% for solid performers (adjusted rate ratio 1.33, CI 1.15-1.54). Top performers (rating 5) are not significantly different from solid performers.
5. **Workload shows only a weak link.** Exit rates rise slightly with weekly hours (17.5% under 40h to 22.2% at 50h+), but the adjusted effect (+3.5% per extra 5 hours) is not statistically significant, and simple tests on hours and overtime are also not significant.

## 5. Caveats

- **Association is not cause.** Pay, tenure, department and workload overlap, and unmeasured factors (manager quality, career progression, personal reasons, labour market) are missing.
- **Voluntary and involuntary exits are combined**, which can blur pay-related patterns.
- **Survivor bias:** people who left before 2023 are not in the data, so early-tenure measures use only hires made from 2023 onward.
- **Small groups have wide uncertainty** (for example Product, HR, and individual roles). Confidence intervals are shown in the dashboard.
- Promotion history is not included: a simple "months since promotion" field is mechanically tied to tenure and gave misleading results in testing, so it needs dated promotion records to be analysed properly.
- The data is synthetic, so effect sizes reflect how it was generated.

## 6. Recommended actions (if this pattern held in real data)

1. **Review pay for people below 0.90 of range**, starting with Sales and Customer Support, where the exit rate is highest. Estimate the cost of replacement against the cost of adjustment.
2. **Strengthen the first year:** structured onboarding, a 30/60/90-day check-in and a buddy or manager review at months 3 and 6-9, when risk is highest.
3. **Target Sales and Customer Support** with a focused retention plan (career paths, workload review, manager coaching).
4. **Look at why low performers leave** (managed exit versus disengagement) before treating this as good or bad turnover.
5. **Track workload as a watch item** rather than a priority until stronger evidence appears.
6. **Improve the data:** record exit reasons (voluntary or not), dated promotions and manager IDs so the next round can separate causes.

## 7. Methods

- **Cleaning:** duplicate removal, range and date consistency checks, missing values kept and flagged or median-filled.
- **Rates:** exits / person-years with exact 95% Poisson confidence intervals.
- **Tenure:** each person's time is split into tenure bands (episode splitting), so people count toward a band only while in it.
- **Tests:** chi-square (categorical) and Mann-Whitney U (numeric) as simple association checks, with effect sizes.
- **Regression:** Poisson regression with a log person-years offset (fitted by IRLS in `src/attrition_lib.py`, cross-checked against scikit-learn). Results are shown as rate ratios.
